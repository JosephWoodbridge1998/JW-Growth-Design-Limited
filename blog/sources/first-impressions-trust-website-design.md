# First Impressions: Trust & Website Design

Issue 05 | JW Growth & Design | 25 October 2026

Make a professional first impression by reducing uncertainty, not adding decoration.

## Reader outcome
Help visitors answer three practical questions: Is this for me? Can I believe it? What happens next?

## Start here
- Read the first screen without scrolling. Is the service obvious?
- Find one piece of evidence that supports a claim.
- Try the next step on a phone and with a keyboard.

A polished design cannot compensate for inaccurate information, weak evidence or a broken contact route. Presentation and substance must work together.

## 01 Lead with relevance, not a slogan
Use a specific headline explaining the service and intended customer or area. Follow it with a short explanation and one primary next step. Leave detailed background further down the page.

## 02 Make the content comfortable to use
Use readable type, clear headings, sensible spacing and sufficient contrast. Avoid text over busy images. W3C recommends visible labels and not relying on colour alone to communicate meaning. [1,2]

## 03 Show evidence a visitor can evaluate
Use real project photos, accurate team information, customer feedback used with permission and qualifications you can substantiate. Identify demonstration work and illustrative outcomes. Never invent scarcity, endorsements or client results.

## 04 Explain the process before asking for commitment
State what happens after an enquiry, what information you need and how pricing or scope is agreed. Distinguish an enquiry from a purchase and a booking request from a confirmed appointment. Avoid response-time promises you cannot keep.

## 05 Make every interaction consistent
Keep button labels specific, navigation predictable and forms clearly labelled. Make error messages useful, preserve entered text where possible and show a clear confirmation. Test keyboard use and contact delivery as well as visual appearance. [2,3]

## Action plan
### LOOK - Check the first screen
Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to explain the offer and next step. Record where they hesitate; do not turn this into an invented conversion statistic.

### VERIFY - Check the supporting evidence
Remove demo details, expired offers and unsupported claims. Match reviews, project descriptions and team information to the real business.

### ACT - Complete the journey
Try the primary action on mobile and with a keyboard. Fix unclear labels, unreadable text, errors and dead ends before adding decorative effects.

Illustrative copy change: instead of "Exceptional solutions for modern living", a gardener might use "Garden maintenance for homes in Reading", followed by the services offered, real work examples and "Request a garden quote". This demonstrates clearer copy, not a measured client result.
