# 5 Signs Your Website Is Costing You Customers

Issue 01 | JW Growth & Design | 25 October 2026

A practical diagnostic for finding friction before committing to a redesign.

## Reader outcome
Find the point where a visitor becomes uncertain, gets stuck or gives up - and decide what to fix first.

## Start here
- Open your homepage on your phone, away from Wi-Fi.
- Find your main service, check your service area and start an enquiry.
- Record the first point where you have to pause, guess or retry.

A warning sign is a reason to investigate, not proof of lost revenue. Check real customer journeys and outcomes before drawing conclusions.

## 01 The page makes visitors wait
Check the homepage and your main service page on mobile. Compress oversized images, remove unnecessary scripts and give media a defined space. Good Core Web Vitals targets are LCP <= 2.5s, INP <= 200ms and CLS <= 0.1 at the 75th percentile of real visits. A lab score alone is not a sales forecast. [1]

## 02 The offer is difficult to understand
Ask someone unfamiliar with your business to explain what you do, who you help and where you work after viewing the page. Rewrite a vague headline as service + location or audience + useful benefit. This is a practical clarity check, not a validated conversion score.

## 03 The mobile journey gets in the way
Check menus, text, cookie controls, phone links and booking buttons. Remove overlaps and horizontal scrolling. Make the primary action easy to locate without relying only on colour; preserve visible focus and readable contrast. [2]

## 04 Visitors cannot judge whether to trust you
Show genuine work, accurate business details, a clear process and permissioned customer feedback. Explain what is included and what affects price. Remove demo addresses, dummy emails and claims you cannot substantiate.

## 05 The enquiry starts but does not finish
Complete a test enquiry with the business owner's permission. Check required fields, useful labels, error messages, confirmation and delivery. Record a successful submission or booking separately from a button click. [3,4]

## Action plan
### FIRST - Remove the failure
Repair broken contact routes, booking links, missing confirmations and intrusive overlays before cosmetic changes.

### NEXT - Reduce uncertainty
Clarify your offer, service area, costs or quoting process and primary next step. Keep the change specific.

### THEN - Measure a useful result
Compare qualified enquiries, completed bookings and delivery failures before and after the change. Note changes in traffic, seasonality and promotions.

Illustrative example: a salon keeps its existing booking provider but replaces a vague "Find out more" button with "Book a haircut", linked directly to the correct booking page. The next check is completed bookings - not whether the button looks nicer.
